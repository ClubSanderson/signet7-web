Sender systems · gateways · recipient systems · evidence tools
Email-first integration model Gmail/SES source pilots

Put verification into the email path.

The source includes signed-email HTTP contracts, local CLI tooling, Gmail pilot tooling, and SES operator tooling. Operators and integrators connect those pieces to transactional, webhook, mailbox, archive, or automation flows; this is not a deployed receive service or public connector program.

Target integration points

An email trust layer, not a replacement mailbox.

01

Sender-side systems

Applications, provider send paths, mail-transfer systems, and secure-email gateways are target integration points for preparing covered components and requesting a signature.

02

Verification controls

Gateways, security products, provider workflows, and recipient clients are target integration points for trust resolution and component-level results.

03

Evidence consumers

Archives, e-discovery, incident response, audit, and case-management systems are target consumers for exported evidence with controlled trust inputs.

04

Identity and key systems

Enterprise identity, key management, DNS, and the planned VSN are target systems for enrollment, discovery, rotation, revocation, and administrative status.

05

Administration

Security and platform teams need tenant-scoped policy, sender inventory, lifecycle controls, monitoring, and evidence operations.

06

Automated consumers

AI agents, workflow automation, compliance pipelines, ERP/CRM systems, and enterprise records consumers may use verified-email evidence before acting. They remain secondary to the email product.

Consumer boundary: AI agents, enterprise systems, workflow automation, ERP/CRM, and records systems are intended consumers and integration targets—not live integrations.

Operator integration track

Source capability must survive the real route.

The candidate profile represents declared headers, MIME structure, bodies, and attachments. Before any compatibility claim, an operator or integrator must wire a named route, capture real provider transformations, exercise negative cases, and retain qualification evidence.

Partner qualification

No logo wall before the integration works.

Required before a compatibility claim

  • A named, versioned integration contract and owner.
  • Fixtures for supported MIME structures and transformations.
  • Positive, negative, revocation, expiry, replay, and recovery tests.
  • Recipient-result and failure-mode review.
  • Operational, security, privacy, and evidence-custody acceptance.

Not established by this repository

  • A signed partnership with any named email provider or vendor.
  • No live qualification or production compatibility with Gmail, SES, Outlook, or another mailbox/provider.
  • Marketplace availability, provider endorsement, or certification.
  • A production SLA, supported deployment, or customer rollout.
  • Universal interoperability with SPF, DKIM, DMARC, S/MIME, or PGP.
Standards relationship

Complement existing controls.

Signet7 is intended to add explicit sender/key trust, declared protected-content integrity, managed lifecycle, and portable evidence. It should be evaluated alongside—not marketed as an automatic replacement for—transport authentication, gateway controls, encryption, reputation, and user safeguards.

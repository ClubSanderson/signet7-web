Trust is a chain of explicit dependencies.

A signature is useful only when the verifier knows what was signed, which key signed it, why that key was trusted, what its status was, and which evidence remains independently controlled.

Trust questions

Five questions, not one green badge.

01

Key control

Did the signature verify under the corresponding public key for the exact covered input?

02

Sender binding

What separately governed relationship connected the key to the claimed sender or organization?

03

Coverage

Which headers, body parts, attachments, and metadata were declared as protected?

04

Status

Was the key active, rotated, revoked, expired, recovered, or disputed at the relevant time?

05

Completeness

Does an independently obtained expected head or anchor support the claimed history?

06

Custody

Who retained the evidence, trust inputs, logs, and policy records—and under what controls?

Signature meaning

Cryptographic validity is not a complete identity claim.

The current s7-email-1 candidate can verify that protected email components match a signed envelope under a corresponding public key. Sender trust still depends on separately governed binding, status, custody, and lifecycle evidence.

ProvesKey control for exact covered bytes under the implemented algorithm and profile.
NeedsEnrollment, organization authority, key custody, lifecycle, and status evidence.
Does not proveLegal identity, title, authority, intent, uncompromised device, or truthful content.
Coverage

Integrity is only as broad as the signed representation.

The candidate source implements deterministic checks for declared protected headers, MIME structure, plain/HTML parts, and attachments under s7-email-1. That source capability does not establish universal email coverage, accepted provider interoperability, or a deployed verification service.

Source-built profile properties

  • Versioned canonicalization and declared component coverage.
  • Protected-header, MIME-tree, part, attachment-byte, and filename checks.
  • Component-level changed, missing, added, reordered, renamed, and invalid outcomes.
  • Resource limits, time/expiry evaluation, and optional replay observation.
  • Primary and independently implemented in-repository verification paths.

Still external or unproved

  • Real-world human identity, authority, intent, or truthful content.
  • No live Gmail/SES transformation and interoperability qualification.
  • Every possible format, gateway rewrite, client rendering, or provider behavior.
  • Independent acceptance, production readiness, certification, or legal admissibility.
VSN trust lifecycle

Verification needs current status, not just a public key.

The Verifiable Sender Network is Signet7's planned managed trust infrastructure for the organizational lifecycle around sender trust. Current source supplies tenant bindings, signed trust events, and optional DNS exact-key matching; it does not prove or operate the complete managed service.

EnrollVerify who may establish the organization/sender/key relationship.
PublishMake key and trust status discoverable through controlled services.
ChangeRotate, revoke, recover, expire, and record administrative actions.
OperateMonitor, support, investigate abuse, and preserve lifecycle evidence.

Coexistence

Different controls answer different questions.

SPF / DKIM / DMARC

Transport/domain authorization and policy signals that remain important. Signet7 does not claim to replace or currently integrate every one.

Gateways / reputation

Threat, abuse, behavioral, content, and policy analysis that can complement cryptographic evidence.

Signet7

Candidate sender/key checks, declared protected-component integrity, and portable evidence; managed lifecycle through VSN remains product direction.

Regulatory context

Durable evidence is a recurring control need, not a universal mandate.

SEC electronic-recordkeeping rules, seven-year SOX-related audit-record retention, six-year HIPAA documentation retention, and EU AI Act logging and retention duties illustrate why covered organizations must preserve records and demonstrate integrity. Applicability depends on the actor, record, role, jurisdiction, and deployment.

Signet7 is not mandated by these regimes. Its cryptographic evidence model is designed to address the underlying need while customer-specific policy, custody, retention, legal analysis, and operational proof remain necessary.
